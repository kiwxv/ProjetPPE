Etymology

Summary

   A word inherited from Germanic.
   A Common Germanic originally strong verb; Old English fléotan (fléat,
   fluton, floten) to float, corresponding to Old Frisian fliata, Old
   Saxon fliotan (Middle Dutch, Dutch vlieten) to flow...    (BUTTON) Show
   more
   A Common Germanic originally strong verb; Old English fléotan (fléat,
   fluton, floten) to float, corresponding to Old Frisian fliata, Old
   Saxon fliotan (Middle Dutch, Dutch vlieten) to flow, Old High German
   flio¥¥an to float, flow (Middle High German vlie¥en, modern German
   fließen to flow), Old Norse flióta (Swedish flyta, Danish fløde) to
   float, flow (not recorded in Gothic) < Old Germanic *fleutan (flaut,
   flutum, flotono-), < pre-Germanic root *pleud-, ploud-, plud- (compare
   Latvian pludinat to float, pludot to flow, pludi flood, Lithuanian
   plústi to float away, pludis float of a fishing-net), an extended form
   of the Old Aryan root *pleu-, plu- (compare Greek plen to sail,
   Sanskrit plu, pru to swim, float, flow, Latin pluere to rain.
